My dissertation combines visual and graph-based representations for character and handwriting recognition in low-resource settings. A scenario where an expert can label a handful of examples per class, and everything else has to be earned from unlabelled data.

The work is funded by the DFG–SNSF project “Combining Image and Graph-based Neural Networks for Handwriting Recognition” (DFG 528122871 / SNSF 217594), a collaboration between the Pattern Recognition in Embedded Systems group at TU Dortmund University and the AIBEX group at the University of Fribourg.

Current threads

Prototype-anchored learning from a handful of exemplars

PAUL trains one autoencoder per class, anchored on one to five expert-selected exemplars, and expands pseudo-labels over an unlabelled pool. The selection criterion is the ratio between the best and the second-best reconstruction error — a sample is claimed only when one class model explains it decisively better than the rest. On EMNIST Balanced this reaches 66.9% accuracy against 53.3% for the strongest baseline, and it holds up on KMNIST and two Omniglot-derived benchmarks against SCAN, DEC, IDEC, JULE and centroid/GMM baselines across DINO, CLIP and convolutional features.

Paper (Best Paper, HIP@ICDAR 2026) · Code

Vision-language models as document readers

Recognition pipelines have segmented pages into lines for decades. Benchmarking nine commercial and open-weight LVLMs across four scripts and three difficulty levels shows that assumption is worth revisiting: page-level input beat line segmentation on every handwritten script we tested, and cost less to run. For Cyrillic, character error rate dropped from 22.6% to 3.9%. The exception is heavily degraded material such as the Bentham collection, where segmentation still earns its place.

SOICT 2025 paper · Journal extension (under review)

Writer retrieval at archival scale

FormWR is a 387,000-page benchmark for writer retrieval where handwriting appears only sparsely on printed forms; closer to what archives actually hold than page-length manuscripts. I contributed X-VLAD, a learned set-aggregation module that compresses a variable set of local descriptors into a 256-dimensional writer embedding. Transferred to HisFragIR20, it reaches 78.8% mAP and 97.9% Top-1, improving on prior published work by +21.6% mAP and +12.7% Top-1.

ICDAR 2026 paper

Information extraction from historical archives

Recognition is rarely the end goal — archives want structured, searchable records. This thread covers layout analysis, entity extraction and record linkage on historical archival material, in interdisciplinary work with historians.
